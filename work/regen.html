03 — The Problem

We carry more battery-powered devices every year, and we walk thousands of steps every day. Almost all of that movement disappears into the ground.

How might we

…turn everyday walking into usable power, without making the shoe look or feel like a gadget?

01

Dead phone, no outlet

Commutes, campuses and travel days drain batteries far from a socket.

02

One more thing to carry

Power banks are heavy, easy to forget, and need charging themselves.

03

Tech that looks like tech

Energy-harvesting concepts tend to look like prototypes, not something you'd wear every day.

04 — Research

Where the foot
does the work.

Before drawing a shoe, I needed to know where the energy actually is, and what people would put up with to collect it.

Method 01

User interviews

Talked with daily commuters and students about when their phones die and what they already carry to prevent it.

Method 02

Pressure mapping

Mapped how load travels through the foot in one step: heel strike, midfoot roll, toe-off. Those three peaks became the pad zones.

Method 03

Benchmarking

Compared power banks, smart insoles and existing energy-harvesting footwear on weight, looks, charging and comfort.

Key insights
  1. Store energy in a part you can remove, so the shoe stays washable.
  2. Pads only earn their place where the foot actually loads.
  3. If it looks like a gadget, people won't wear it every day.

06 — CAD & Prototyping

Four layers.
One job each.

Scroll to pull the insole apart.

  1. Layer 01

    Knit top cover

    Breathable and removable, so the insole can be washed.

  2. Layer 02

    Piezo harvesting pads

    Heel, midfoot and forefoot, matched to the pressure map.

  3. Layer 03

    Flex circuit

    Carries charge back to the heel dock's pogo-pin contacts.

  4. Layer 04

    Cushioned base

    Protects the pads and keeps the ride comfortable.

07 — Final Design

stored.

3 zones
