03 — The Problem

Riders depend on their helmet more than any other piece of gear, yet many put up with one that feels heavy, runs hot and fogs up when it matters.

How might we

…design a full-face helmet that protects properly, stays comfortable on long rides, and looks as sharp as the bike?

01

Heat and fog

Visors fog and shells trap heat in slow traffic.

02

Weight and fatigue

Every gram is felt on the neck after an hour.

03

Fit and pressure

Generic liners leave pressure points on different head shapes.

04 — Research

What riders
actually feel.

I looked at how riders choose, wear and live with their helmets before deciding what this one should do differently.

Method 01

Rider interviews

Asked daily commuters and weekend riders what they like, and put up with, in their current helmets.

Method 02

Benchmarking

Compared full-face helmets on visor systems, venting, weight and shell construction.

Method 03

Fit and ergonomics

Studied head forms, field of view and how the liner and shell layers share the work.

Key insights
  1. Riders judge a helmet by its visor first: clarity, tint and how easily it swaps.
  2. Comfort comes from fit and the liner, not just from padding.
  3. Graphics matter. Riders want a helmet that looks like it belongs on their bike.
